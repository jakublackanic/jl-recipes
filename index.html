Greek Yogurt Naan for a Pizza Oven

Soft, blistered restaurant-style naan made with full-fat Greek yogurt and a long cold ferment, optimized for a high-temperature pizza oven.

  • Cuisine: Indian
  • Category: Bread
  • Yield: 6 large naan
  • Prep: 30 minutes
  • Cook: 10 minutes
  • Total: 18 hours 40 minutes

Ingredients

  • 500 g bread flour or strong 00 flour
  • 110 g full-fat Greek yogurt
  • 150 g whole milk
  • 100 g water
  • 15 g neutral oil or melted ghee
  • 12 g sugar
  • 10 g fine sea salt
  • 2.5 g baking powder
  • 1 g instant yeast
  • 60 g ghee or unsalted butter, melted
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely grated (optional)
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander, finely chopped (optional)
  • 1 tsp nigella seeds (optional)
  • Fine sea salt, for finishing

Instructions

  1. Combine the flour, sugar, salt, baking powder and instant yeast. Add the Greek yogurt, milk, water and oil or ghee. Mix until no dry flour remains.
  2. Knead for 6–8 minutes until smooth, soft and slightly tacky. Avoid adding extra flour unless the dough is truly unmanageable.
  3. Cover and leave at room temperature for 45–60 minutes, then refrigerate for 12–24 hours.
  4. Divide into 6 pieces of about 150 g. Shape into smooth balls, cover and leave at room temperature for 1.5–2.5 hours, until relaxed and slightly puffy.
  5. Fully preheat the pizza oven. Aim for a stone temperature of 400–430 °C. Once the stone is saturated with heat, reduce the flame to low before launching.
  6. Lightly flour the bench. Stretch or roll one dough ball into a teardrop or oval about 3–4 mm thick. Press in nigella seeds if using. Avoid heavily flouring the underside.
  7. Launch toward the front or cooler part of the oven. Bake for about 60–90 seconds, rotating every 15–20 seconds. If the top needs more colour, flip for another 15–30 seconds.
  8. Mix the melted ghee or butter with garlic and coriander if using. Brush over the hot naan, add a pinch of salt, and stack under a clean towel to keep it soft.

Pizza oven notes

Target a stone temperature of 400–430 °C. The oven can reach 450 °C, but a 450 °C stone is not recommended for naan — the underside sets and scorches before the crumb opens.

Fully preheat so the stone is heat-saturated, then reduce the flame before launching. The naan should ride a soaking-hot stone under a gentle flame, not a roaring one.

Same-day option

Skip the cold ferment

Use 3 g instant yeast instead of 1 g. After kneading, ferment for 1–1.5 hours, divide and ball, rest another 45–60 minutes, then bake.

The 12–24 hour cold-fermented version above is the default and gives better flavour and blistering.
