Pho Bo

Hanoi's beef noodle soup: a clear broth from charred ginger, onion and toasted spice, poured boiling over raw sliced beef and flat rice noodles.

  • Cuisine: Vietnamese
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 6 bowls
  • Prep: 45 minutes
  • Cook: 6 hours
  • Total: 7 hours

Ingredients

  • 2 kg beef leg and knuckle bones
  • 1 kg oxtail or beef shin
  • 2 large onions, unpeeled and halved
  • 100 g ginger, unpeeled and halved lengthways
  • Spices: 5 star anise
  • Spices: 1 cinnamon stick
  • Spices: 5 cloves
  • Spices: 1 tbsp coriander seed
  • Spices: 1 tsp fennel seed
  • Spices: 3 black cardamom pods
  • 60 ml fish sauce, plus more to taste
  • 40 g yellow rock sugar
  • Fine sea salt
  • 600 g fresh flat rice noodles, or 350 g dried
  • 400 g beef eye round, sliced paper thin against the grain
  • Spring onions, coriander and white onion, sliced, to serve
  • Thai basil, bean sprouts, lime and bird's eye chilli, to serve

Instructions

  1. Cover the bones with cold water, boil hard for 10 minutes, then drain and rinse each bone clean. This is the single step that decides whether the broth is clear or cloudy.
  2. Blacken the onion and ginger directly over a gas flame or under a hot grill, about 10 minutes, until the cut faces are charred and the kitchen smells sweet. Rinse off the loose ash.
  3. Toast the star anise, cinnamon, cloves, coriander, fennel and black cardamom in a dry pan for 2 minutes, until fragrant. Tie them in muslin.
  4. Cover the clean bones with 6 litres of cold water. Bring slowly to a bare simmer and hold it there for 5 hours. Never let it boil, and skim the surface for the first 30 minutes.
  5. Add the charred onion and ginger, the spice bag, fish sauce and rock sugar for the final 90 minutes.
  6. Strain through muslin. Season with salt and more fish sauce. The broth should taste slightly too strong on its own, because noodles and beef will mute it.
  7. Blanch the noodles in boiling water for 20–30 seconds, drain hard and divide between warmed bowls.
  8. Lay the raw beef slices over the noodles in a single layer, add the sliced onion and herbs.
  9. Return the broth to a rolling boil and ladle it over. The heat of the broth cooks the beef in seconds. Serve with the herb plate, lime and chilli on the side.

Never let it boil

Pho broth should stay clear enough to read the pattern on the bottom of the bowl. A boil churns fat and protein into permanent suspension and clouds it for good.

Aim for the surface barely shivering, around 85–90 °C. Crack the lid, or move the pot half off the burner, to hold it there.

Slicing the beef

Thin enough to cook in the bowl

Freeze the eye round for 45 minutes before slicing. Firm meat lets you get to 1–2 mm with a normal knife, which is the thickness that cooks through on contact.

Thicker slices stay raw in the middle as the broth cools. If your slices come out chunky, dip them in the boiling pot for 5 seconds before they go in the bowl.
