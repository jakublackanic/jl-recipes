Tonkotsu Ramen

Kyushu's opaque pork bone broth, boiled hard for ten hours until the collagen and fat emulsify, served with chashu, marinated egg and thin straight noodles.

  • Cuisine: Japanese
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 4 bowls
  • Prep: 1 hour
  • Cook: 12 hours
  • Total: 26 hours

Ingredients

  • Broth: 2 kg pork trotters, split
  • Broth: 1 kg pork neck or back bones
  • Broth: 1 onion, halved
  • Broth: 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
  • Broth: 30 g ginger, sliced
  • Chashu: 800 g pork belly, rolled and tied
  • Chashu: 200 ml soy sauce
  • Chashu: 200 ml mirin
  • Chashu: 100 ml sake
  • Chashu: 50 g sugar
  • Tare: 120 ml of the chashu braising liquid
  • Tare: 2 tbsp light soy sauce
  • Tare: 1 tsp fine salt
  • 4 eggs, at room temperature
  • Aroma oil: 60 ml neutral oil
  • Aroma oil: 6 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 600 g fresh thin straight ramen noodles
  • 4 spring onions, finely sliced
  • Nori, bamboo shoots and white pepper, to serve

Instructions

  1. Cover the trotters and bones with cold water, bring to a boil and hold for 10 minutes. Drain and scrub every bone under running water until no grey scum remains. Skipping this makes the broth taste muddy and stay grey.
  2. Return the clean bones to the pot with fresh water to cover by 5 cm. Bring to a rolling boil and keep it there for 10 hours, topping up with boiling water to hold the level. Unlike every other stock, this one must never be gentle.
  3. Add the onion, garlic and ginger for the final 2 hours.
  4. Meanwhile simmer the rolled belly in the soy, mirin, sake, sugar and 400 ml water for 3 hours, turning hourly, until a skewer meets no resistance. Cool it in the liquid, then chill overnight before slicing.
  5. Boil the eggs for exactly 6 minutes 30 seconds, chill in iced water, peel, and steep overnight in 200 ml of the braising liquid let down with 200 ml water.
  6. Strain through a colander, pressing the softened bones through. Return to the heat and reduce until it coats a spoon and looks like single cream. Expect about 2 litres.
  7. Fry the sliced garlic in the oil over a low heat until deep gold, then blend or crush it into the oil.
  8. Warm the bowls with boiling water. Put 2 tbsp of tare and 1 tbsp of aroma oil in each, then pour in 400 ml of very hot broth and stir.
  9. Boil the noodles for 60–90 seconds, drain hard, and lay them in the broth without tangling.
  10. Sear the chashu slices briefly, then top each bowl with pork, a halved egg, spring onion, bamboo and nori. Serve at once.

The rolling boil is the recipe

Tonkotsu is white because violent agitation shears the rendered fat into droplets that collagen then holds in suspension. It is an emulsion, built by turbulence.

Simmer it politely and you get a thin, clear, brownish stock. Good stock, wrong dish. Keep it at a genuine boil for the full 10 hours.

Splitting the work

A realistic two-day plan

Day one: purge and start the broth, braise the chashu, marinate the eggs. Chill the broth overnight, which also makes the surface fat easy to lift and add back in measured amounts.

Day two: reduce the broth, make the aroma oil, slice the chashu and assemble. Under an hour of active work.
