Hyderabadi Chicken Biryani

Marinated chicken and part-cooked basmati sealed in one pot and steamed on dum, so the rice finishes in the aromatics rising from the meat below.

  • Cuisine: Indian
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 6 servings
  • Prep: 40 minutes
  • Cook: 1 hour
  • Total: 3 hours 40 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 kg bone-in chicken, in large pieces
  • 500 g aged basmati rice
  • Marinade: 250 g thick full-fat yogurt
  • Marinade: 20 g ginger paste
  • Marinade: 20 g garlic paste
  • Marinade: 2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
  • Marinade: 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • Marinade: 2 tsp garam masala
  • Marinade: 3 tbsp lemon juice
  • Marinade: 20 g fine salt
  • Marinade: 1 large bunch coriander and mint, chopped
  • 300 g onions, very thinly sliced
  • 300 ml neutral oil, for frying the onions
  • 60 g ghee
  • Whole spices: 4 green cardamom, 2 black cardamom, 1 cinnamon stick, 4 cloves, 2 bay leaves
  • A generous pinch of saffron, in 60 ml warm milk
  • 2 tbsp rose water or kewra water (optional)
  • Dough of 200 g flour and water, to seal the lid

Instructions

  1. Fry the sliced onions in the oil over a medium heat for 20–25 minutes, until evenly deep brown. Drain on paper. They will crisp as they cool. These are the birista, and they carry most of the sweetness in the dish.
  2. Mix the chicken with the yogurt, ginger, garlic, chilli, turmeric, garam masala, lemon juice, salt, herbs and half the fried onions. Refrigerate 2 hours, or overnight.
  3. Rinse the basmati until the water runs clear, then soak 30 minutes and drain.
  4. Boil a large pot of heavily salted water with the whole spices. Add the rice and cook for exactly 5 minutes, until the grains are soft outside but still hard at the centre. Drain immediately.
  5. Spread the marinated chicken and all its liquid in a heavy pot. Cover with the drained rice. Scatter over the remaining fried onions, the ghee, the saffron milk and the rose water.
  6. Roll the dough into a rope, press it around the rim and clamp on the lid. If you have no dough, use two layers of foil under a tight lid. The seal is what makes it dum, not a pilaf.
  7. Cook on a high heat for 5 minutes to get the base steaming, then move to the lowest possible heat, on a heat diffuser if you have one, for 35 minutes.
  8. Take it off the heat and rest, still sealed, for 15 minutes.
  9. Crack the seal at the table. Dig down through every layer with a wide spoon so each portion gets rice, chicken and gravy. Do not stir.

The five-minute par-boil

The rice enters the pot roughly 70% cooked and finishes on trapped steam. Boil it to done and the dum stage turns it to paste.

Test by pressing a grain between your fingers: it should crush at the ends and resist in the middle. Aged basmati holds this margin far better than new-crop rice.

Raw or cooked chicken

Kacchi versus pakki

This is the kacchi method, where raw marinated chicken cooks under the rice. It gives the best texture, and it depends on pieces of even size, bone-in, so nothing dries out.

Nervous about timing? Brown the marinated chicken for 10 minutes first, then layer and cut the dum to 25 minutes. That is the pakki method, and it is far more forgiving.
