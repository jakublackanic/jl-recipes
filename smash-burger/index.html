Smash Burger

Thin patties pressed hard onto a screaming cast iron surface for maximum crust, with American cheese, pickles and a two-minute burger sauce.

  • Cuisine: American
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 4 double burgers
  • Prep: 15 minutes
  • Cook: 12 minutes
  • Total: 27 minutes

Ingredients

  • 800 g beef chuck mince, 20% fat, cold
  • 8 slices American cheese
  • 4 potato buns, split
  • 1 white onion, sliced paper thin
  • 16 dill pickle slices
  • Sauce: 4 tbsp mayonnaise
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp tomato ketchup
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp yellow mustard
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp pickle brine
  • Sauce: half a teaspoon of smoked paprika
  • 40 g unsalted butter, for the buns
  • Flaky salt and black pepper

Instructions

  1. Whisk the mayonnaise, ketchup, mustard, pickle brine and paprika. Refrigerate until needed.
  2. Divide the cold mince into 8 loose balls of about 100 g. Handle them as little as possible and do not season yet. Salt drawn into the meat early dissolves protein and makes the patty bouncy.
  3. Butter the cut faces and toast them in a dry pan until deep gold. Set aside.
  4. Heat a cast iron pan or flat plancha over the highest heat for 5 minutes, until a drop of water skitters and vanishes. Ventilate the kitchen.
  5. Put two balls in the dry pan, well apart. Within 10 seconds, press each flat to about 5 mm with a stiff spatula and hold for 10 seconds. Season the top with salt and pepper.
  6. Cook undisturbed for 90 seconds, until the edges are lacy and brown and the crust releases on its own.
  7. Scrape under the patty with a sharp-edged spatula so the crust stays with the meat. Flip, lay on a slice of cheese, and cook 30 seconds more.
  8. Stack two patties per bun with onion and pickles. Sauce both bun faces. Repeat with the remaining balls and serve straight away.

Why the crust needs a dry pan

The browning is a Maillard reaction, and it needs direct metal contact above roughly 150 °C. Added oil pools under the patty and steams it instead.

20% fat mince renders enough of its own fat within seconds. Leaner beef sticks and dries out, so do not go below 15%.

Two thin patties, not one thick

Crust-to-meat ratio

Every patty is mostly crust and a little interior. Two 100 g patties give twice the browned surface of one 200 g patty at the same weight.

Cook a maximum of two at a time. A crowded pan loses heat, and the beef greys and steams instead of searing.
