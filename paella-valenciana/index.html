Paella Valenciana

The original Valencian paella: chicken, rabbit and beans over a wide thin layer of bomba rice, never stirred, finished with a toasted socarrat on the base.

  • Cuisine: Spanish
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 4 to 6 servings
  • Prep: 20 minutes
  • Cook: 50 minutes
  • Total: 1 hour 10 minutes

Ingredients

  • 400 g bomba or Calasparra rice
  • 1.2 litres chicken stock, hot
  • 600 g chicken thighs and drumsticks, in pieces
  • 400 g rabbit, in pieces
  • 150 g flat green beans (ferradura), topped and halved
  • 100 g butter beans or garrofo beans, cooked
  • 1 ripe tomato, grated
  • 100 ml olive oil
  • 2 tsp sweet pimenton
  • A generous pinch of saffron threads
  • 1 sprig fresh rosemary
  • Fine sea salt
  • Lemon wedges, to serve

Instructions

  1. Toast the saffron in a dry pan for 20 seconds, crumble it into 3 tbsp of the hot stock and leave it to infuse.
  2. Heat the oil in a 42 cm paella pan. Salt the chicken and rabbit and brown them slowly over a medium heat for 15–20 minutes, turning, until deeply coloured. Push the pieces to the rim.
  3. Fry the green beans in the centre for 5 minutes, then move them out to the rim as well.
  4. Lower the heat. Add the grated tomato and cook it down for 5 minutes until it darkens and no longer smells raw. Stir in the pimenton and take it off the heat within 15 seconds, because paprika burns almost instantly.
  5. Add the hot stock, the saffron infusion, the butter beans and the rosemary. Boil hard for 10 minutes, then taste and season firmly. This is the last moment you can correct the salt.
  6. Scatter the rice evenly across the whole pan and shake it level. From this point on, never stir. Stirring releases starch and turns paella into risotto.
  7. Boil hard for 10 minutes, then reduce to a bare simmer for 8 minutes more. Rotate the pan over the burner so the heat reaches the edges evenly.
  8. When the liquid is gone, raise the heat to high for 60–90 seconds. Listen for a faint crackle and stop the moment you smell toast, not smoke.
  9. Cover with a cloth and rest 5 minutes off the heat. Serve from the pan with lemon.

Rice depth is the whole technique

The rice layer should be no more than 1.5 cm deep, which is why the pan is so wide. A deep layer steams and goes gluey because the top grains never meet the heat.

Too small a pan? Cut the recipe down rather than piling the rice higher. Judge by pan diameter, not by appetite.

Liquid ratio

Three to one, by volume

Bomba absorbs about three times its volume in liquid, roughly double what long grain takes. Substituting basmati or arborio here fails in different but equally reliable ways.

Wide pans evaporate fast. Keep 200 ml of hot stock in reserve, and add it only around the edges if the rice is still chalky when the pan runs dry.
