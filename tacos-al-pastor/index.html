Tacos al Pastor

Chile and achiote marinated pork, stacked on a skewer and roasted until the edges char, served with pineapple, onion and coriander on corn tortillas.

  • Cuisine: Mexican
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 18 tacos, 6 servings
  • Prep: 45 minutes
  • Cook: 1 hour 30 minutes
  • Total: 14 hours 15 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1.4 kg pork shoulder, in 5 mm slices
  • 200 g pork fat or thick-cut bacon, sliced
  • 6 guajillo chiles, stemmed and seeded
  • 3 ancho chiles, stemmed and seeded
  • 2 chiles de arbol, for heat (optional)
  • 30 g achiote paste
  • 150 g fresh pineapple, plus 400 g to grill
  • 80 ml white vinegar
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, Mexican if possible
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • Half a teaspoon of ground cloves
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 20 g fine sea salt
  • 10 g sugar
  • 36 corn tortillas, doubled up
  • 1 white onion, finely diced
  • 1 large bunch coriander, chopped
  • Limes, to serve
  • Salsa verde or salsa roja, to serve

Instructions

  1. Toast the guajillo and ancho chiles in a dry pan for 30–45 seconds a side, until they smell nutty and turn pliable. Do not let them blacken or the marinade turns bitter.
  2. Cover the chiles with just-boiled water and soak 20 minutes. Keep 150 ml of the soaking liquid.
  3. Blend the drained chiles with the achiote, 150 g pineapple, vinegar, garlic, oregano, cumin, cloves, pepper, salt, sugar and the reserved liquid until completely smooth. Pass through a sieve, pressing hard.
  4. Toss the pork slices and the pork fat in the marinade so every surface is coated. Cover and refrigerate 12–24 hours.
  5. Thread the slices onto two long metal skewers, alternating pork with the fat, packing them tight and flat into a dense cylinder about 15 cm across. Finish with a wedge of pineapple on top.
  6. Roast at 200 °C for 75–90 minutes, standing the skewers upright in a loaf tin so the fat bastes downwards. The stack is done at a core temperature of 70 °C.
  7. Raise the heat to maximum, or move under a hot grill, for 8–10 minutes until the outside is dark and crisp at the edges.
  8. Rest 10 minutes. Carve thin shavings down the outside of the stack with a long knife, taking only the crisp layer. Return the stack to the grill between rounds to re-char.
  9. Warm the tortillas on a dry comal until flexible and lightly spotted. Stack them in a towel.
  10. Fill each doubled tortilla with pork, a little grilled pineapple, onion and coriander. Lime and salsa at the table.

No skewer, no problem

The plancha method

Skip the stack. Sear the marinated slices in a single layer on a screaming-hot cast iron pan, 90 seconds a side, then chop them on the board with the grilled pineapple.

You lose the basting, so add 2 tbsp of lard to the pan. The flavour is most of the way there in a quarter of the time.

Why the pineapple

Fresh pineapple carries bromelain, an enzyme that breaks down protein. In the marinade it tenderises the shoulder, which is why the slices stay soft after a long roast.

The same enzyme turns meat mushy past about 24 hours. Do not extend the marinade to two days, and do not use tinned pineapple, where the bromelain has been cooked away.
