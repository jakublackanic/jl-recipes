Spaghetti alla Carbonara

Rome's four-ingredient pasta: guanciale, egg yolk, Pecorino Romano and black pepper, emulsified off the heat into a glossy sauce that never scrambles.

  • Cuisine: Italian
  • Category: Main
  • Yield: 4 servings
  • Prep: 10 minutes
  • Cook: 15 minutes
  • Total: 25 minutes

Ingredients

  • 400 g spaghetti or rigatoni
  • 150 g guanciale, in 5 mm batons
  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 1 whole egg
  • 100 g Pecorino Romano, finely grated
  • 2 tsp black peppercorns, coarsely cracked
  • Coarse salt, for the pasta water

Instructions

  1. Put the guanciale in a cold, dry pan and bring it slowly to a medium heat. Render 8–10 minutes until the fat has run and the batons are golden but still yielding. Take the pan off the heat and reserve the fat.
  2. Toast the cracked pepper in a dry pan for 30 seconds, until fragrant.
  3. Cook the pasta in well salted water, but use only about two thirds the usual volume of water so it ends up starchier.
  4. Whisk the yolks, whole egg, Pecorino and pepper into a thick paste. Loosen it with 2–3 tbsp of the warm guanciale fat.
  5. Whisk a ladle of hot pasta water into the egg mixture, a little at a time. This is the step that decides the dish: warming the eggs gradually stops them setting on contact.
  6. Drain the pasta 1 minute short of the packet time and tip it into the guanciale pan, off the heat. Pour over the tempered egg and toss hard and continuously for about a minute.
  7. Loosen with pasta water until the sauce coats each strand and pools slightly. It thickens as it cools, so serve it looser than you think.
  8. Plate at once, with more Pecorino and pepper.

The temperature that matters

Egg yolk begins to set around 65 °C and is firmly scrambled by 73 °C. Carbonara is the act of holding the sauce in the narrow band below that while starch and cheese thicken it.

If the pan is on the flame, you have already lost. Toss off the heat, and if the sauce stays too loose, return the pan to a switched-off hob for 20 seconds at a time.

Substitutions, honestly

Pancetta works and tastes good, but it is a leaner, different dish. Smoked bacon takes it somewhere else entirely.

There is no cream in carbonara. The gloss comes from yolk, fat and starch. Cream is what people add when the emulsion breaks.
